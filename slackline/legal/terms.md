## 1. The App

1.1 Slackline lets you communicate with your own agents from Slack. Each agent you connect receives a thread in your direct messages with Slackline. Your replies in that thread wake the agent, and the agent responds in the thread. You may also choose channels in which an agent listens.

1.2 You sign in with Slack, and you do not need a Wakeshift account. Adding Slackline to Slack, signing in, and approving an agent each constitute your acceptance of these Terms. Next to the **Add to Slack** and **Sign in with Slack** buttons, on the step that explains where your messages go when you first sign in, and on the page where you approve an agent, Slackline states "By continuing, you agree to Slackline's Terms and Privacy Policy", with links to these Terms and to the Slackline Privacy Policy.

## 2. Adding Slackline to Slack

2.1 You may add Slackline only to a Slack workspace in which you are authorized to do so. Your Slack administrator may need to approve it, as Slack's own settings determine.

2.2 Slack's terms govern your use of Slack, and your organization's rules for Slack apply.

2.3 Removing Slackline from Slack disconnects every person's agents in that Slack workspace and deletes what Slackline keeps for it.

## 3. Your Agents

3.1 **Your agent acts for you.** When you approve an agent on Slackline's approval page, the agent acts on your behalf. You choose its name, and it posts under that name, identified as your agent. You may rename or remove it at any time on your Slackline page.

3.2 **Who can reach your agent.** In your direct messages, only you can reach your agent. In a channel, only you can reach it, unless you set the channel to "from anyone there". Slackline asks you to confirm that setting first and notifies the channel. Guests and people outside your organization can never reach your agent.

3.3 **Messages from others.** If you allow anyone to reach your agent, you are responsible for what your agent does with their messages. Your agent runs with your access, and other people's messages may attempt to cause it to take actions you did not intend. Slackline marks those messages as coming from someone else, but that cannot prevent every attempt. People in the channel must not use your agent to do anything that you could not ask it to do yourself.

3.4 **Channel rules.** A channel rule lasts only while you are a member of the channel. When you leave the channel, your agents stop listening there. If your Slack account is deactivated, your agents are removed.

3.5 **Limits on agents.** Your agent cannot mention @channel, @here, people or groups, and cannot read anything in Slack beyond the messages Slackline delivers to it.

## 4. Where Your Messages Go

4.1 You must have the right to send the messages your agent receives to that agent and its agent provider. Your organization's rules and your Slack administrator determine whether you have that right.

4.2 You must keep model training turned off for every agent and agent provider that you connect to Slackline, so that no message from Slack is used to train an AI model.

4.3 We never use Slack data to train AI models, and we do not sell it or share it beyond delivering it to the agent you chose. The [Slackline Privacy Policy](/privacy) describes who receives it and for how long.

In addition to Section 5 of the Terms for Our Apps, you must not use an agent to post spam, to post as someone else, or to reach channels or people that you could not reach yourself. We may remove agents, channel rules or a Slack workspace's connection that breach this Section 5.
